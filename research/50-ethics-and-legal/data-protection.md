# Data protection — what governs us

*Posture: document, don't decide. This file records what the applicable frameworks say and what questions they raise; it does not authorise any personal-data processing.*

## Nepali law

- **Individual Privacy Act, 2075 (2018)** and its Regulation (2077/2020) — Nepal's primary privacy statute; covers personal information held by public bodies, requires consent for collection/disclosure, criminal penalties for breach. `[UNKNOWN — needs verification: how it treats emergency/humanitarian processing; no explicit humanitarian exemption is known to us]`
- **Constitution of Nepal (2015), Art. 28** — right to privacy.
- **Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Act, 2074 (2017)** — establishes NDRRMA and the authority structure for disaster response; incident data reporting to BIPAD runs through Nepal Police. `[UNKNOWN — needs verification: whether it grants any data-sharing powers during emergencies]`
- Practical implication: any system holding missing-person records for this event almost certainly needs a Nepali public body (NDRRMA, Nepal Police, or Nepal Red Cross under its Movement mandate) as the accountable data controller. A foreign volunteer team cannot legitimately be the controller of Nepali disaster victims' data.

## Humanitarian norms (harder than law in practice)

- **ICRC Handbook on Data Protection in Humanitarian Action** (reference text) — purpose limitation, data minimisation, basis-for-processing analysis even where law is silent, heightened care for vulnerable people, restrictions on data sharing with authorities that could harm data subjects.
- **IASC Operational Guidance on Data Responsibility in Humanitarian Action** — data-responsibility diagnostics per data flow.
- **OCHA Centre for Humanitarian Data guidance** on statistical disclosure risk — aggregate data can still re-identify people in small settlements. Relevant to any building-level damage × population overlay we produce: at village scale, "the house with 6 residents that was destroyed" is identifying.
- **Sphere / Core Humanitarian Standard** — accountability to affected populations, not just to responders.

## The data-controller question (open)

For anything beyond fully non-personal geospatial work, we need answers to:

1. Who is the data controller? (Not us — see above.)
2. What is the lawful basis and the humanitarian legitimate basis?
3. What is the retention/deletion plan when the response ends?
4. Who can affected people complain to?

Until these have answers, this team's scope is **non-personal data only**: terrain, imagery, infrastructure, aggregate settlement-level exposure. That scope is deliberately chosen so useful work can proceed *without* touching these questions.
