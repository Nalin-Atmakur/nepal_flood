# Morgue and disaster-victim-identification backlog

## Summary

The Kathmandu Post reports a severe body-processing and identification bottleneck across central Nepal. At the temporary Bharatpur centre, 233 remains had been received by 16:00 on 28 August, while photographs and records had been prepared for only 82. Temporary facilities lacked refrigeration, remains were being transferred between districts, families could not easily travel because roads and bridges were gone, and at least one heavily affected district had no certified forensic pathologist. Authorities were relying on formal identifiers including fingerprints and DNA where visual identification was impossible.

## Extracted claims

- As of 16:00 on 2026-08-28, the Bharatpur temporary centre reported records/photos prepared for 82 of 233 recovered bodies: a concrete intake backlog.
- Temporary morgues had been opened in community halls and police compounds; some lacked adequate refrigeration.
- Road and bridge destruction prevented some families from reaching facilities to inspect records or provide reference samples.
- Nawalparasi East reportedly had six staff able to perform basic autopsies but no certified forensic pathologist and was seeking help from Kathmandu.
- Remains were being moved among facilities, increasing the importance of authoritative identifiers and transfer records.
