# Day-four rescue operations and specialist-support requests

## Summary

AP's day-four field report describes continued helicopter evacuation and tunnel rescue in Nuwakot and Rasuwa. Poor weather temporarily stopped aerial operations. At the Upper Trishuli-1 worksite, the Army said deep mud and very few helicopter landing places were obstructing the rescue; teams also had to relocate temporarily after a secondary-flood warning. The foreign minister said Nepal had sought specialist help in tunnel rescue, survivor-detection technology, rapid forensic/DNA analysis, and refrigerated mortuary capacity. Indian tunnel specialists had arrived and a Chinese team was expected.

## Extracted claims

- As of 2026-08-29, poor weather had temporarily hampered helicopter operations in Nuwakot before flights resumed.
- As of 2026-08-29, the Army described roughly 1.2–1.5 m of mud at the Upper Trishuli-1 site and a limited number of helicopter landing places.
- Rescue teams had temporarily moved out of the worksite after warnings of possible further flooding.
- The Government of Nepal had explicitly requested specialised help for tunnel rescue and survivor detection, plus rapid forensic/DNA processing and morgue refrigeration.
- An 11-person Indian tunnel-rescue reconnaissance team had deployed; a Chinese team was expected.
