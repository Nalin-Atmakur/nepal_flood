# Evidence file: the missing-persons information flow is fragmented

## Summary

Consolidated evidence that the bootstrap brief's predicted failure mode — fragmented missing-person information — is happening in this event. Recorded to inform the build/no-build decision; **no personal data extracted**.

## Extracted claims

- **How families actually search (27–29 Aug):** physically at Kathmandu hospitals (National Trauma Center, TUTH, Maharajgunj) — printed photo noticeboards, photos washing off in rain and being reprinted; police/volunteer desks logging searchers; QR codes routing to the Police unidentified-bodies DB (ABC Australia 29 Aug; Al Jazeera 27–28 Aug). At the Trishuli barracks hub. Digitally: high-volume social appeals; a WhatsApp group of families of missing US citizens (CNN 27 Aug); embassy phone/WhatsApp lines.
- **Official lists diverge:** Nepal Police released missing names 28 Aug; Nepal Tourism Board's traveler register (644–668 depending on day) was **not updated after 26 Aug** per Reuters factboxes; Home Ministry/NEOC aggregate counts differ again; security forces track their own missing separately. Counts across publishers on overlapping days: 826 / 1,924 / ~2,500 / 644 / 667.
- **Parallel unofficial lists are leaking PII:** a trade outlet republished a 655-row private table with names, ages, phone numbers; it links onward to another private tracker of unknown provenance. (Deliberately not linked here; see LINKS_INDEX policy.)
- **Unadopted registries get no users:** a self-styled "national registry" site held 5 public cases on day 4.
- **The identification bottleneck is physical, not digital:** bodies recovered across ≥7 districts up to ~240 km downstream; morgues over capacity (temporary 250-body centre in Bharatpur); cold storage buys 10–14 days; unidentified remains to be buried with tag numbers + coordinates within 1–2 weeks (Kathmandu Post 28 Aug).
